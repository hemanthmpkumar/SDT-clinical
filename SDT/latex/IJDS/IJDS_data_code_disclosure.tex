\subsection*{3. Data Availability and Simulation Disclosure}

This study reports a computational data science methodology and simulation analysis in healthcare operations and sequential decision processes. \textbf{No real human subjects, live patient records, or protected health information (PHI) were collected, accessed, or analyzed.}

All experimental evaluations, multi-modal interaction states, and sequential diagnostic trajectories were generated via controlled in-silico simulation:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
    \item \textbf{Simulated Diagnostic Vignettes:} $N = 1{,}000$ standardized synthetic clinical scenarios spanning Critical Care ($n=264$), Emergency Medicine ($n=250$), Hospital Medicine ($n=245$), and Internal Medicine ($n=241$). The cases are stratified into monomorphic presentations ($n=469$) and multimorbid presentations ($n=531$) with acute simulated diagnostic pivots.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Interaction Corpus:} A standardized synthetic benchmark corpus ($N = 546{,}028$ simulated documents and records) incorporating continuous physiological waveform tokens, discrete clinical events, and semi-structured text fragments generated for algorithmic benchmarking.
    \item \textbf{Simulated Decision Sessions:} $9{,}000$ simulated workflow runs evaluating time-to-correct-information (TTCI), retrieval accuracy, and cognitive load metrics under a randomized Latin-square crossover design.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Ethical Oversight Statement:}
Because this study relies entirely on in-silico computational simulations and public algorithmic benchmark schemas without human subjects, live animals, or real patient medical records, Institutional Review Board (IRB) review and informed consent requirements were not applicable.

\textbf{Replication Data and Simulation Artifacts:}
All synthetic vignette generators, simulation scripts, and evaluation data logs will be deposited in the official \textit{IJDS Supplemental Archive} upon final acceptance.

\subsection*{4. Software and Algorithmic Artifacts for Post-Acceptance Archival}

Upon final acceptance, the complete algorithmic suite of Spectral Diagnostic Trajectories will be archived under an open-source permissive license (\textbf{MIT / Apache License 2.0}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Core Spectral Algorithms}:
    \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}\setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
        \item Multi-modal hypergraph construction and sparse incidence operators ($H_t, W_t, D_v, D_e$).
        \item Implicitly restarted Lanczos eigensolver for dynamic Fiedler tracking ($\lambda_2, v_2$) in $\mathcal{O}(|E_t|)$.
        \item Chebyshev polynomial graph high-pass filtering ($H(L_t)$) and Cheeger bisection partitioning.
        \item Biased global random walk engine and Sweller cognitive knapsack greedy solver.
        \item Analytical Erlang C ($M/M/c$) queueing sensitivity and congestion simulation module.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Software Environment}: Python $\ge 3.9$, PyTorch $\ge 2.0$, SciPy $\ge 1.10$, NetworkX $\ge 3.0$, NumPy $\ge 1.24$. Deterministic execution is guaranteed via fixed random seed (\texttt{seed=42}) and Conda/pip lockfiles (\texttt{environment.yml}).
\end{enumerate}
