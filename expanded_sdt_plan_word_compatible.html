Expanded Research Plan: Spectral Diagnostic Trajectories (SDT)

Executive Summary

This document outlines the next evolutionary phase of the Geodesic Diagnostic Trajectories (GDT) framework. While GDT successfully models clinical intent as a continuous trajectory on the Riemannian manifold of Symmetric Positive Definite (SPD) matrices S_d^+, its reliance on sliding-window covariance bounds its ability to natively ingest highly unstructured, continuous, and multi-modal physiological streams.

The Spectral Diagnostic Trajectories (SDT) framework transitions the state space from a Riemannian manifold to a dynamic multi-modal hypergraph. It replaces the geodesic shift ratio κ_t with spectral gap analysis (Fiedler value λ_2) for pivot detection, and replaces JEPA-driven latent prefetching with spectrally-biased random walks over the global EHR graph.

Phase 1: Hypergraph Construction & Multimodal State Space

Objective: Encode the physician's cognitive path and the patient's physical state into a unified, continuous hypergraph topology, building upon the differential geometry established in GDT.

Node Initialization and Embedding Formulation

  • The raw query or clinical action initiated by a clinician at turn t is denoted as q_t.
  • Textual & Semantic Nodes: Ingest clinical notes, query logs, and discrete EHR events. These are processed into a dense embedding e_t = Encoder(q_t) ∈ ℝd using models like ClinicalBERT. To maintain computational efficiency, the original 768-dimensional embeddings are projected down to a 128-dimensional subspace using a learned linear layer, preserving the semantic geometry established in GDT.
  • Physiological Nodes: Expand the state space to ingest continuous data (e.g., MIMIC-IV PPG-ECG waveforms, HiRID continuous vitals). These nodes represent high-frequency physiological states encoded via 1D-CNN or state-space models (e.g., Mamba).

Covariance Elicitation and Baseline State Tracking

  • As a foundational bridge from GDT, the local semantic intent at turn t is initially represented by a symmetric positive definite (SPD) matrix computed over a sliding window:
    S_t = (1/k) ∑i=t-k+1t (e_i - μ_t)(e_i - μ_t)T + λI_d
    where k is the sliding window size, μ_t represents the local mean of the embeddings, and λI_d strictly guarantees numerical stability and positive definiteness.
  • The state matrix S_t resides in the Riemannian manifold of SPD matrices S_d^+, equipped with the affine-invariant metric tensor:
    g_S(u,v) = Tr(S-1u S-1v)
  • The evolving diagnostic uncertainty is quantified through the differential entropy of the Gaussian density:
    H(S_t) = (1/2) log det(2πe S_t)

Evolution to Hyperedge Formation

  • While GDT relies entirely on the covariance matrix S_t, SDT transitions this into a dynamic active subgraph G_t = (V_t, E_t) to accommodate the multimodal nodes.
  • Unlike GDT's pairwise covariance, hyperedges can connect multiple nodes simultaneously.
  • Edges are weighted based on a combination of the clinician's temporal clickstream sequences (sequential proximity) and cosine similarity in the latent embedding space.
  • The instantaneous diagnostic hypothesis is thus continuously tracked as this evolving active subgraph G_t, expanding upon the foundational Riemannian state tracking.

Phase 2: Pivot Detection & The Interference Gate (GDT to SDT Evolution)

Objective: Mathematically detect abrupt changes in diagnostic intent to trigger the interference gate, transitioning from GDT's differential geometry to SDT's spectral graph theory.

The Legacy GDT Geodesic Trigger

In the foundational GDT model, sudden cognitive pivots were detected by measuring the acceleration of the state matrix S_t across the Riemannian manifold.

  • Geodesic Distance: The distance between consecutive states is measured as:
    d_g(St-1, S_t) = ||log(St-1-1/2 S_t St-1-1/2)||F
  • The Shift Ratio (κ_t): To differentiate between normal evidence accumulation and an abrupt pivot, GDT calculates the ratio of the current step to the historical moving average:
    κ_t = d_g(St-1, S_t) / [ (1/k) ∑i=1k d_g(St-i-1, St-i) ]

The SDT Spectral Trigger Evolution

While the geodesic shift ratio κ_t is highly effective for discrete semantic embeddings, it becomes computationally unstable when applied to continuous, high-frequency physiological nodes. SDT resolves this by mapping the pivot detection to the eigenvalues of the evolving hypergraph G_t.

  • Clique Expansion (Bridging the Topology): To resolve the structural gap between the multi-modal hypergraph and the pairwise Laplacian, the adjacency matrix A_t is derived via clique expansion. For a hypergraph with hyperedges E_t and weights W(e), the standard edge weight between nodes i and j is defined as:
    At, ij = ∑e ∈ E_t | i,j ∈ e [ W(e) / (|e| - 1) ]
  • Laplacian Monitoring: At each time step t, compute the normalized graph Laplacian matrix for the active subgraph G_t using the expanded adjacency matrix:
    L_t = I - D_t-1/2 A_t D_t-1/2
    where D_t is the degree matrix.
  • Fiedler Value Tracking (λ_2): Monitor the spectrum of L_t, specifically the second smallest eigenvalue, known as the Fiedler value λ_2(L_t). Steady growth represents corroborating evidence.
  • Spectral Interference Gating: A sudden, precipitous drop in λ_2 mathematically indicates that the active subgraph is fracturing into distinct clusters. The interference gate triggers when the spectral gap expands rapidly:
    Δλ_2 > τpivot

Phase 3: Graph-Signal Context Attenuation

Objective: Bound context contamination and prevent stale hypotheses from polluting downstream trajectory simulation by executing a mathematically rigorous Fiedler cut.

  • Spectral Filtering: Upon trigger activation, a high-pass graph filter H(L_t) is applied to actively suppress low-frequency structural components associated with the pre-pivot stale context.
  • Subgraph Decoupling (The Fiedler Cut): The system isolates the structural fracture using the Fiedler vector v_2. The graph is mathematically partitioned into two disjoint sets based on the algebraic sign of the Fiedler vector components:
    Vstale = {i ∈ V_t | v_2(i) < 0}
    Vactive = {i ∈ V_t | v_2(i) ≥ 0}
  • Severing Edges: All hyperedges connecting Vstale and Vactive are systematically severed, providing an instantaneous bound on information interference.
  • Embedding Formulation: The cleanly filtered nodes in Vactive are aggregated to generate the seed for the next query state.

Phase 4: Anticipatory Prefetch Engine & Simulation Interface

Objective: Evolve the retrieval engine into a predictive world model driven by optimal control constraints.

  • Optimal Control Formulation: The system optimizes a continuous loss function J(u_t) that balances temporal retrieval delay, structural relevance, and cognitive load bounds:
    J(u_t) = E[ αℒdelay(Gt+1, u_t) - βℛstruct(u_t) + γΩ(u_t) ]
  • Spectrally-Biased Random Walk: The system computes a transition probability mapping nodes into a spectral embedding space φ(v) to weight trajectories structurally similar to the active subgraph:
    P(v_j | v_i) = [ P_0(v_j | v_i) exp(-τ ||φ(v_i) - φ(v_j)||22) ] / [ ∑k ∈ N(i) P_0(v_k | v_i) exp(-τ ||φ(v_i) - φ(v_k)||22) ]
  • Cognitive Gating: The prefetch volume is mathematically restricted to prevent overwhelming the clinician's cognitive bandwidth:
    ∑v ∈ u_t H(v | G_t) ≤ Cmax

Phase 5: Evaluation & Operational Analytics

Objective: Validate SDT and quantify the downstream operational impact on hospital throughput.

  • Crossover Benchmark Metrics: Time-to-Correct-Information (TTCI), Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR), and Contextual Precision.
  • Queueing Model Translation (M/M/c): To measure system-wide impact, the clinical time saved is mapped into an Erlang C queueing model representing Emergency Department (ED) operations.
  • With an accelerated service rate μ' = 1 / (μ-1 - Δt), the ED traffic intensity updates to ρ' = λ / (cμ').
  • Impact on Wait Time (W_q): The projected reduction in patient queueing wait time is evaluated as:
    W_q = [ P_W · (μ')-1 ] / [ c(1 - ρ') ]
